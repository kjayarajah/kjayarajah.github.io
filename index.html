I'm an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at New Jersey Institute of Technology. My core research interests lie in the areas of mobile and ubiquitous sensing, deep learning for IoT and the edge, mobility-driven urban analytics and predictive cyber-physical systems.

In my most recent work, I’m exploring the concept of "Collaborative AI-based sense-making" for resource-limited edge devices (e.g., infrastructural cameras, autonomous systems, wearable computers, etc.) where groups of sensors, due to their proximity and shared perspectives of the sensed environment, can cooperate for performance benefits. Previously, I explored the use of localization technology in improving the operations of built environments, both at the indoor-scale and city scale. I have worked on a variety of predictive urban applications (e.g., predicting non-conformant behavior, mobility-on-demand simulations/public transportation, consumer marketing and retail survivability), building on both social and behavioral insights derived from individual and group mobility.

Recent Projects

ConCord: Toward Human–Robot Cooperative Search (ACM MobiSys'26)

How can robots complement the way people search? ConCord explores how wearable sensing can give robots insight into human attention and search behavior. By connecting gaze, head, and hand movements with robotic systems, we lay the groundwork for teammates that recognize what people have explored and where assistance may be needed. Our prototype spans simulated and real-world search environments, taking a step toward more adaptive human–robot collaboration.

Fluency in Gesture-based Teleoperation (IEEE/RSJ IROS'24, IROS'26)

We explore how hand gestures can enable intuitive mobile robot teleoperation. Our real-time system translates hand movements captured by a head-mounted device into robot motion commands through various gesture schemes that offer different tradeoffs between precision and intuitiveness. User studies show that gesture-based control performs comparably to traditional interfaces, even when participants perceive their performance differently. Extending this work, we infer operator fluency from motion smoothness and hand-joint features to predict task completion time and corrective actions with high accuracy.

Collabrative Perception at the Edge (ACM SenSys'26 and IEEE INFOCOM'26)

Networked camera systems increasingly process video at the edge to reduce communication and energy overheads, but lightweight, edge-friendly models face an undesirable complexity–accuracy tradeoff. SteerCam jointly adapts camera viewing angles and selective sharing of hints between inference pipelines, improving detection as objects move through the monitored space. FusionBridge extends collaborative perception to static RGB cameras and mobile LiDAR-equipped robots, using compact, class-aware hints to overcome challenges such as occlusion and poor lighting.
